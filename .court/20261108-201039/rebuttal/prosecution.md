# Rebuttal: Prosecution

1. **Hardware Simulation Activates Board Syntax**: Does not survive. While `board "<id>"` blocks exist, emulating three distinct microcontrollers (RP2040, ESP32, micro:bit) with virtual circuitry in-browser introduces massive peripheral emulation debt that a solo, pre-launch developer cannot shoulder alongside four other expansions.

2. **Visual Scrubbing and Live Rooms Form an Educational Suite**: Does not survive. Coupling omniscient time-travel debugging across two dialects and three disparate execution tracks with distributed WebRTC/CRDT multi-cursor rooms compounds delivery risk, especially when the record is completely silent on any state serialization format or signaling infrastructure.

3. **Dedicated Small Model Makes Browser AI Usable**: Does not survive. While smaller downloads are desirable, the record reveals zero training data, corpus size, or compute budget for this pre-launch language, rendering custom sub-50MB model distillation an operational impossibility that will waste scarce resources.

4. **WASI 0.2 Unlocks the Existing WASM Backend**: Does not survive. Implementing WASI 0.2 Component Model binaries and `wit` interfaces on a self-hosted WebAssembly seed VM prematurely targets external cross-ecosystem interop before the core toolchain's three divergent execution tracks have even launched.

**The One Point for the Jury to Decide:**
The jury must decide whether a single self-funded student developer, prior to launch, can simultaneously execute five disjoint, enterprise-scale domains—hardware emulation, time-travel debugging, neural model distillation, WASI component standards, and P2P networking—without guaranteeing total delivery paralysis.
