## Charges that stuck
- **Simultaneous scope overload:** Attempting five massive initiatives at once will exhaust a solo, self-funded student and stall launch (8 jurors).
- **Unverified feasibility and budget:** The record contains no financial plan, delivery timeline, or compute budget for five complex systems (4 jurors).
- **Architectural fragmentation:** Concurrently building disjoint subsystems without shared state serialization or clear focus creates runaway technical debt (2 jurors).

## What the defense got right
- **Architectural continuity:** Every feature builds logically upon working foundations, including board syntax, the WebAssembly compiler, and the WebGPU engine.
- **Compelling educational vision:** In-browser tools like visual debugging and zero-hardware circuit simulation directly strengthen the beginner-friendly Prism dialect.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. **Abandon simultaneous rollout:** Replace the multi-front expansion with a phased roadmap that launches the stable core language first.
2. **Select one wedge feature:** Focus all development bandwidth on shipping one high-impact feature—such as the visual debugger—shelving the rest.
3. **Establish a lean budget and sandbox:** Define a zero-cost delivery schedule and local execution boundaries before attempting distributed networking or model distillation.

## The sentence
Halt all parallel expansions immediately. You have built an impressive, self-hosted compiler; do not bury it under five unlaunched architectures. This week, publish a sequenced roadmap that shelves four features, protects your wallet, and commits every hour to delivering your core language to real users.
